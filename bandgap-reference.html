MOSFET-Only Subthreshold Bandgap Voltage Reference

A temperature-stable voltage reference built without bipolar transistors, designed and simulated in SKY130 for a UWASIC Tiny Tapeout submission.

Status: pre-layout. The transistor-level reference works from −40 °C to 120 °C in schematic-level simulation.

At a glance

A bandgap reference built from subthreshold MOSFETs and simulated in SKY130. It holds about 630 mV to within 9 mV from −40 °C to 120 °C and rejects supply noise by about 65 dB at low frequency. Its main weakness is at higher frequency: rejection falls off, and the supply-to-output response rises slightly above 0 dB around 100–200 kHz, which points to the amplifier bandwidth and compensation.

Problem

Every analog block on a chip needs a voltage that does not move when the temperature or the supply does. We set out to build that reference from a MOSFET-only subthreshold topology, so the design does not depend on process-specific bipolar devices.

The reference runs from a 1.8 V supply, targets more than 40 dB of PSRR, and is characterized from −40 °C to 120 °C in SKY130. It is one component of a larger UWASIC chip design.

Circuit architecture

In weak inversion a MOSFET's drain current depends exponentially on its gate-source voltage, the same way a BJT's current depends on VBE. Two devices of different size carrying the same current therefore differ in VGS by a voltage that is proportional to absolute temperature (PTAT). A single VGS, meanwhile, falls with temperature (CTAT). Adding a weighted PTAT term to the CTAT term flattens the output.

ΔVGS = η · VT · ln N   N = 8: M1 is eight times the size of M0
I = ΔVGS / R0
VREF = VGS0(T) + I(T) · (R1 + R3)
How the loop closes, step by step
  • Two branches hang from the VREF node. The left branch is R3, R1, R0 and M0. The right branch is R4, R2 and M1, where M1 is eight times the size of M0.
  • The error amplifier compares the mid-nodes of the two branches (the R3/R1 and R4/R2 junctions). Because R3 = R4, forcing those nodes equal forces the two branch currents equal.
  • The amplifier's output drives PMOS M7, which supplies both branches from VDD. VREF is M7's drain.
  • With equal currents, M0 and M1 differ in VGS by ΔVGS. R0 turns that into the PTAT current, and R1 + R3 turn the current back into a voltage that adds to VGS0.
  • A capacitor (C1) in series with a resistor (R5) links the amplifier output to VREF for Miller compensation.
  • The circuit has a zero-current state, so in simulation a short current pulse (I0) kicks it out of that state.

SKY130 device characterization

We measured the SKY130 devices before sizing anything, instead of reusing constants from the original reference design. Sweeps of the 1.8 V NMOS (nfet_01v8) gave three numbers the design equations depend on:

  • Subthreshold slope factor: η, from the slope of the log-current curve.
  • CTAT slope: ∂VGS/∂T ≈ −0.63 mV/°C, from a temperature sweep of a diode-connected device.
  • Weak-inversion limit: for a unit-size device (W/L = 1/1) the log-current curve stays straight up to roughly 3 nA. Above that the exponential model stops holding. The limit scales with W/L.

Error amplifier

The amplifier is a five-transistor differential stage: NMOS input pair M3/M4, PMOS current-mirror load M5/M6 and NMOS tail source M2. We designed and checked it on its own testbench before connecting it to the core. The first DC run found the tail source M2 in triode; lowering its bias voltage put it in saturation, and every device then passed the region check.

Standalone, it has 41 dB of low-frequency gain and a −3 dB bandwidth of a few hundred kilohertz.

Hand calculation

  1. Identify the nodes: the inputs are the gates of M3 and M4, and the output is the drain of M6, which drives the gate of M7.
  2. Pick a design point of 1.8 V supply, about 10 µA of tail current and roughly 150 mV of overdrive, so each side carries 5 µA and gm = 2ID/Vov ≈ 67 µS.
  3. Start with L = 1 µm and estimate gain from Av ≈ gmro, aiming for 40 to 60 dB.
  4. Check that every device stays in saturation, then move to simulation.

DC transfer and AC response

A DC sweep of one input around 500 mV moved the output from about 1.7 V down to about 0.1 V, a slope near −105 V/V, or about 40 dB. The AC response confirmed it.

The standalone testbench biases M2 from a DC source. In the full reference, M2 shares its gate node with core device M0, so the amplifier is biased by the core. The standalone numbers are a starting point, not the amplifier's in-circuit behavior.

Integration and debugging

Putting the core and the amplifier together took a few passes.

  1. The first combined temperature sweep rose with temperature, so the PTAT term was too strong. Increasing R0 to weaken it changed almost nothing, so we temporarily took out output device M7.
  2. Sweeping the NMOS showed how little current it can carry and stay in weak inversion, so we redid the calculations. An intermediate iteration still leaned PTAT, with 12.4 mV of total variation.
  3. Continued tuning of the resistor values brought the variation down to about 9 mV over −40 to 120 °C.

Results

All results are pre-layout ngspice simulations of the schematic above.

Temperature stability

VREF sits near 630 mV and moves about 9 mV peak to peak from −40 °C to 120 °C: 636.7 mV at −40 °C, a minimum of 627.6 mV around 70 °C, and about 630 mV at 120 °C. That is an effective temperature coefficient of about 89 ppm/°C (9 mV / (0.63 V × 160 °C)). The curve is a shallow bowl: first-order cancellation removed the linear drift, and the second-order curvature that remains sets the 9 mV.

Power-supply rejection

At low frequency the supply-to-output gain is about −65 dB, so PSRR ≈ 65 dB at 1–10 Hz, better than the 40 dB target. It stays above 40 dB up to roughly 600 Hz and reaches 0 dB (no rejection) around 75 kHz. It peaks at about +2 dB in the 100–200 kHz range, where supply noise is slightly amplified (PSRR ≈ −2 dB), and settles near −3 dB above about 1 MHz. A bump like this is common when the error amplifier has limited bandwidth or the compensation is not fully tuned. A wider bandwidth would help high-frequency PSRR, but it has to be balanced against stability.

Behavioral Verilog-A model

Mehak built a behavioral Verilog-A model of the reference for system-level simulation, compiled with OpenVAF and run in ngspice. With parameters extracted from the transistor-level circuit, it gives VREF = 632.9 mV at 27 °C and about 65 dB of low-frequency PSRR.

It is deliberately first order: a straight-line temperature dependence and a flat PSRR. Set beside the transistor-level results, it misses the second-order curvature and the +2 dB supply-response peak around 100 kHz, which come from the amplifier and compensation dynamics the model leaves out.

Design methodology

We sized the reference from SKY130 characterization instead of values taken from the original design. That makes the method repeatable: swap the PDK, re-run the device sweeps, and re-derive the weighting.
